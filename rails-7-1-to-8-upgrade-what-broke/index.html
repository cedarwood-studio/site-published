Measured answer

What broke when a 27,000-line Rails app went from 7.1 to 8.0, and how long each stage took

We upgraded Postal, an open-source mail server, from Rails 7.1.6 to 8.0.5.2 on a fork, in four stages, each reviewed by an automated pass of our own process, and recorded what broke and how long each step ran. The breaks listed here cover the whole planned path to 8.1, and the two worst were in code the test suite never runs. The four stages took 81 minutes of machine time to build and 109 minutes to review. That's machine time with no person's time in it, and it isn't a calendar estimate.

One gem blocked the path. annotate, a development tool, caps Active Record below 8.0 at its latest version, and stage 2 removed it. The dependency resolver found a working set of gems for every step, on 8.0 and 8.1 by downgrading annotate.

The first automated pass found four of the eight. A review found the other four, and one of them had already shipped in a finished stage. That story has its own page.

The report's 25.1 minutes is 8.1 in the sandbox, 15.1 working out the suite and writing, and 1.9 reading the prose. The sums are ours, added from the four stage rows. Stage 3's 14.9 minutes is from the first run and its fixes are from the second.

On stage 3, review took longer than building. As first built it took 14.9 minutes and passed every check, and it didn't start with one feature switched on. It took five review rounds and four fixes before a review passed it.

D

What this doesn't tell you

  • How long your upgrade takes in calendar time. These are machine minutes for four of ten stages on one app. The report's own estimate for all ten stages is 30 to 263 hours of an engineer's attended time, and nothing has calibrated it. The two numbers don't measure the same thing.
  • What a harder app looks like. Postal sits at the easy end, with one blocking gem of 162 and a fast suite. An app with a dozen blocked gems and thin tests would look very different.
  • The riskiest stages. Rails 8.1 and the defaults stages, which touch cookies, sessions and how stored data is serialized, haven't run.
  • That nothing else is broken. Each of the last three review rounds on stage 3 found a smaller difference than the one before. We can't say how many are left.
  • What a senior Rails engineer would add. The reviews here are passes of our own process. No person has reviewed the sample yet.
  • Anything in production. Everything ran in a sandbox, with no real configuration, data or traffic.
