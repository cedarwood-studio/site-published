About the studio

Cedarwood Studio builds new web apps for businesses, and finishes and secures apps made in app builders such as Lovable, Replit, Bolt and Base44 and gets them ready to launch. For teams with an application in production, it also finds what a Rails or Node.js upgrade will break before they commit to it, and why a GitHub Actions pipeline is slow and which of its jobs are flaky. Each service has a fixed price, in writing before you pay, and a senior software engineer in the San Francisco Bay Area answers for it.

Last updated November 27, 2026

A

How the work is done

A new app is built, and an app-builder app finished, to a written scope. The checks you'll use to accept the work are agreed in that scope before work starts. Each one has an automated test, and you try every one yourself on a private demo before the second half is due. Much of the build is automated, and a person reviews everything before it's delivered.

Upgrade work runs on a software factory built for upgrades and infrastructure. It holds hundreds of upgrade rules, each drawn from the official release notes and documentation for a specific version step, and a sandbox that installs your application and runs its own test suite. For an upgrade we carry out, it does this at each stage and also compares the running application before and after, feature by feature, and doesn't pass a stage with unexplained differences.

Every report is carefully reviewed before it's delivered. On an upgrade we carry out, each stage receives both automated and professional review passes before it reaches you.

Each report comes with the data behind its numbers, so you can verify it yourself.

B

How your code is handled

  • We ask for the least access the work needs, read-only where possible, and never for production credentials.
  • For an Upgrade Roadmap, any code we run goes in a container that holds no credentials.
  • AI tooling processes what you send us and the code and materials you give us access to, in whole or in part, from your first message on. The AI is Anthropic's Claude, with model training on our data turned off. Any other AI tool is named to you in writing before it touches your material.
  • We delete our copies when you ask.
  • What passes through an AI provider is held there under its retention policy, which we don't control.

The terms have the full version, under how we handle your code and data.

D

Who answers for the work

Jack Senechal, a senior software engineer in the San Francisco Bay Area, owns the studio and answers for every job it delivers.

From 2017 to 2023, Jack was an engineering manager and principal software engineer at Mavenlink (now Kantata). He took over its integrations platform when it was unreliable and cut its error rate roughly 100x. He then moved that platform from Heroku to Google Cloud with a zero-downtime cutover.

He later inherited a one-person DevOps team after mass departures and rebuilt it. Over several years he led the move of the company's Rails monolith and its services onto Kubernetes, and he designed and operated its CI/CD pipelines.

In 2024 he was an infrastructure engineer at Tobiko, where he built the infrastructure for the company's hosted product on Google Cloud and built and maintained its CI/CD pipelines. He has a BA in mathematics from the University of North Carolina at Asheville.
