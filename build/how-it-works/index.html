Custom web apps

How a custom web app build works

A build runs to a written scope at a fixed price, from your first email to the code in a repository you own. This page is the detail behind the custom web app page, and the terms have the full rules.

Last updated November 28, 2026

01

What are the steps?

Six steps, by email apart from the walkthrough. We work with businesses only.

  1. Your email. A few lines on what the app is for and who will use it, sent to hello@cedarwood.studio. Please don't send real customer records at this stage.
  2. Questions both ways. We reply with a few questions, none of them technical, each with what we'll assume if you're not sure, so "fine" is a complete answer. Until you accept a scope, there's no charge for this.
  3. A written scope with a fixed price. The features in your words, the acceptance checks you'll try yourself, the production-ready list below, the delivery date, the price and any add-ons with their prices. You accept it by email, and from then on the price and the scope change only through a change order that you accept in writing, and the date moves only for that or for delays on your side.
  4. The build. You pay the first half by card through Stripe when you accept the scope. We build the app in our own private repository and run it on a private demo with made-up data, so none of your accounts are needed.
  5. Delivery and a walkthrough. We walk you through every acceptance check on the demo, recorded for you to keep, and you try them yourself. One round of changes is included. The second half is due once every check passes and the work is accepted.
  6. The handover. We transfer the repository to you, and you put the app live in accounts in your name with our step-by-step guide and our help by message. The 30-day fix window starts that day.
02

What does "production ready" mean, item by item?

Each of these is delivered, and the handover document shows the evidence for each. Your scope lists them for your app.

  • Tests for everything you'll check. Every acceptance check in your scope has an automated test that does what the check describes, in a real browser. The handover says which test covers which check.
  • Tests on every change. They run on their own each time the code changes, and a change that fails them is flagged before it goes live.
  • Security checks. Each kind of user can see and change only what the scope says, tried against the server directly as well as through the screens. No passwords or keys in the code. Every library the app builds on is checked for known security problems. Forms refuse bad input, the app is served over HTTPS only, and repeated failed sign-ins are slowed down.
  • Errors handled and logged. When something fails, people see a clear message instead of a crash page. The error is logged with enough detail to find the cause, and without passwords or card details.
  • Backups that work. Automatic database backups are part of the setup, and we restore one on the demo before delivery to show it works.
  • A deployment you own. You put the app live in accounts in your name, with you as the top administrator, following our written guide. You type every password and key yourself, and we never ask for them.
  • A handover document. How the app fits together, how to run the tests and ship a change, every account it uses and what each costs, how to restore a backup, and what to do if it stops working.
  • A walkthrough. We walk you through every acceptance check on the demo, recorded for you to keep.

The security checks are part of our own delivery checklist. They aren't a security audit, a penetration test or a certification.

03

What is it built on?

By default, TypeScript, running on Cloudflare. Cloudflare has a free plan that's enough for many small apps. The database and any other services are chosen in your scope, which names every service the app uses and what each costs.

The code is standard TypeScript, in a repository you own, and any developer who knows TypeScript can work on it. The handover lists every open-source library in it with its license. If you need a different language, framework or host, say so in your first email, and we'll tell you whether we can build it that way, and at what price, before you commit.

04

What will it cost to run?

Hosting, the domain, email sending and payment fees are billed to you directly by those providers, in accounts in your name. Your scope estimates each one before you pay, and the handover lists every account and what it costs.

05

How does it go live?

Every build comes with a written step-by-step guide to putting the app live in your own accounts, and during the fix window we answer your questions by message while you follow it. More help is optional, and your scope names each item with its price before you pay.

  • A live deployment call. We stay on a video call, for up to the time your scope states, while you put the app live, following the guide with you. You share your screen, and you pause the share while you paste a password or key.
  • Account setup and hand-off. We create a new hosting account for your business, put the app live there and hand the account over to you. Until the hand-off we hold its access. At the hand-off you become its owner with your own sign-in, our access ends, and we confirm that in writing. You then add the keys of your other accounts, such as payments or email, yourself.
  • Installation on hosting you already have. If you'd like the app on hosting or systems you already run, we tell you first whether it can run there, then adapt it and the guide to them and take you or your developer through the installation. It's billed by the hour at the rate in your scope, up to a cap you approve before we start, and it sits outside the fixed price and its refunds. A defect in the app's own code that also shows on the demo, or when the app is put live as our guide says, is still covered by the fix window.
06

Who owns the code?

You do, once it's paid for: the code, the repository with its history, the tests and the documents. We transfer the repository to you within 2 business days of the second payment, and the terms say what happens to our copies after that.
