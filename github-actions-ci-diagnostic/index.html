A

What you'll know afterward

Where the time goes
How long a run takes and what it costs in runner minutes, and which jobs and steps the time sits in.
How often it fails
Your failure rate and your re-run rate, and which jobs and steps fail.
What's flaky
The jobs that failed and then passed on the same commit, and the failing tests where the logs name them.
What else is costing you
Dependency caches that aren't doing their job, and third-party actions that have become a risk.
What to change first
The changes ranked by what they save, each with how big the change is. Where the top one is a speed fix that can be tested on a fork, we test it there and show the runs before and after. For a private repository that takes a fork you give us access to. If there's little to change, the report says so. We never need write access to your own repository.
B

What's in the report

  • The short version. The handful of numbers to read first, and the changes in order.
  • Time to a verdict. The median and the 90th percentile on your main branch and on pull requests, the runner minutes a run uses, and the time by job and by step.
  • Failures and re-runs. The failure rate on your main branch and on pull requests, the re-run rate, and the jobs and steps that fail most.
  • Jobs that failed and then passed on the same commit. Each one is listed. For pytest and unittest, the tests the logs name are ranked, each with its error line and a link to the log.
  • Third-party actions. Each action your workflows use, and whether it's pinned to a fixed version, archived or on a deprecated runtime.
  • Dependency caches. Which jobs have one, and how often it's hit.
  • The changes, ranked. Each with what it saves, how big the change is and the arithmetic behind the saving.
  • The tested fix. Where the top change is a speed fix that can be tested on a fork, the runs before and after.
  • What the history couldn't settle. Anything your data was too thin to show, and why.
  • The data. Each number comes with the data it was computed from, so your engineers can check it. The report comes in Markdown and as one HTML page, with the computed results and the stored data.

In scope: your GitHub Actions run history and workflow files. Out of scope: finding the cause of a flaky test or fixing it, and your application code.

C

What two public pipelines showed

We ran the Diagnostic on two open-source projects and published both reports.

  • The first pipeline was already well kept. The report still found a three-line change that cut the median run by more than a third on a fork.
  • The second is a pipeline where people press re-run. In 30 days, 62 runs were re-run after a failure, and the report names and ranks the tests the logs name.

A team that waits on CI every day has a guess about where the time goes. The report gives you your own numbers and an order to work in.
