Not Just Today

Not Just Today shows a whole year of Google Calendar on one screen. Trips, vacations, school breaks and other events that run for days appear as bars across the twelve months, and the one-hour meetings are left out. It's free to use at notjust.today.

It's the studio's own product. We designed and built it, and we run it in production.

What we built

The app
TypeScript in the browser, with a small server on Cloudflare. The page, its scripts and its styles come to 23.6 KB compressed, and a check on every change keeps them under 25 KB.
Sign-in
With Google. It asks for two read-only permissions, the list of calendars and the events on them, so it can't add, change or delete anything.
Privacy
The events are kept in the browser on the device, so the year draws at once next time. The server passes them along and keeps none of them. To keep the year up to date, it keeps only the access Google grants, encrypted, and Disconnect deletes it. There's no analytics, advertising or tracking.
Screens
All twelve months on a laptop or a big monitor, and three months at a time on a phone.

Built to stay open all day

A year view is a tab people leave open, so we measured what that costs. The account was synthetic and heavy, with 15 calendars and 5,000 events. The figures are from Chrome on our own machine, before and after a performance pass, on November 26, 2026.

In a soak test simulating about 40 hours left open, with 300 refreshes, 200 year changes and 100 trips to a background tab, the app's page elements, listeners and stored events stayed flat, and none of the app's own objects grew in memory.
