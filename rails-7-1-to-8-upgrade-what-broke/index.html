Measured answer

What broke when a 27,000-line Rails app went from 7.1 to 8.0, and how much work each stage took

We upgraded Postal, an open-source mail server, from Rails 7.1.6 to 8.0.5.2 on a fork, in four stages, each put through a separate review pass, and recorded what broke. Eight lines were flagged as breaking on the path to 8.1, and the two worst were in code the test suite never runs. The stage that moved to Rails 7.2 passed every check when it was first built. It took four fixes and five rounds of review before a review passed it.

One gem blocked the path. annotate, a development tool, caps Active Record below 8.0 at its latest version, and stage 2 removed it.

A first pass found four of the nine. A separate review of it found the rest, and one of them had already shipped in a finished stage. How that happened has its own page, along with what it says about judging an upgrade's risk before you start.

The report's estimate for all ten stages of this app is an effort range of 30 to 263 hours of an engineer's time.

As first built, stage 3 passed every check, and it didn't start with one feature switched on. The review caught that in its first round, and the stage went back four times before a review passed it. The reviews counted here are review passes, a step of our process.

D

What a browser saw

Tests and recorded responses don't show what a person sees, so we also loaded the running app in a browser on Rails 7.1.6 and on the fourth stage, Rails 8.0.5.2, and went through it feature by feature with the same data, at a desktop and a phone width. No screen broke in the 116 states that render a page. The other two of the 118 are file downloads.

MeasureValue
Features, screens and screen states compared12, 31 and 118

Six of the nine changes are error text. In the test environment a missing page answers with the framework's backtrace, and the version numbers in it changed. Two are a few pixels at the corner of a form control, and they didn't come back on a second capture. That second capture showed the same few-pixel flicker on two other screens, which is why we read it as the browser's own noise. One is six pale pixels at the corner of a select box on one screen, invisible at normal size, and it showed on both captures of the new version.

Both sides ran in the test environment, so the error pages a production user sees weren't compared. We ran the comparison on November 18, 2026.

E

How far this goes

This is one app, and it sits at the easy end, with one blocking gem of 162 and a fast test suite. Four of the ten planned stages have run. The ones still to run, Rails 8.1 and the framework defaults, touch cookies, sessions and how stored data is serialized.
